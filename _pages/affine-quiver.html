Reading the explorer

Enter the words

n counts the nodes of the affine Dynkin diagram of type An−1(1). Enter generators 1 through n. Both n and 0 denote s0. Spaces or commas separate letters. For n ≤ 9, compact words such as 123123 also work. Use e or an empty field for the identity. The explorer accepts 3–24 nodes and up to 48 letters per word.

The target v must be reduced and feasible in β. Positive β enables both constructions, including nonreduced braids. Negative letters in β switch to the signed plabic diagram.

Reading direction and word moves

In Leftmost mode, the paper reads the entered word from left to right and selects the leftmost reduced subexpression for the element v. The plabic construction uses the reflected wiring, including reflected cut numbers and reversed arrow orientation. Rightmost mode restores the earlier convention. Switching modes recomputes the seed for the words in the form.

An adjacent braid relation or commutation appears as a bracket above the wiring. Click a bracket to apply its verified word change, or choose a relation to preview the new word first. A fully solid three-letter braid move is a square mutation. Other local relations may preserve the quiver up to relabeling. Click μ at any mutable vertex to preview the abstract quiver mutation. The relation menu identifies which previews have an adjacent word realization. Undo retraces applied word changes.

Follow a disk

A solid bridge at word position d gives one quiver vertex and one soap film. Select it on the diagram or in the film menu. Choose Trace from birth to follow the film toward the boundary. In Leftmost mode it starts at cut d and advances to the right. In Rightmost mode it starts at cut d−1 and advances to the left. A cut numbered c lies after the first c displayed letters. Reaching the outer boundary makes a film frozen.

Top and Bottom show the two projections. Lifts shows integer heights before reducing modulo n. Several heights can cover the same face, so a film can contribute more than once. The 3D view shows the periodic strand diagram schematically, with film coverage on the two coordinate walls.

Step through Bao–Ye

Next move advances one commutation, square move, freeze or deletion. A commutation moves the barred letter past its neighbor and preserves the seed up to relabeling. A square move swaps the matching generators and mutates the quiver in the same step. Freezing updates the neighboring vertices together. Deletion removes the marked vertex and shows the graph for the new partial target.

Choose Play to follow the moves in order or Replay operation to watch just the selected Bao–Ye operation. The lower slider selects one of its moves. After a square move, Before swap and After swap let you compare the diagrams and quivers. Full graph cycles shows how the pivot boundary reverses and how its neighbors change in that same mutation.

Local signed word shows the moving barred generator on the auxiliary word for the current stage. The displayed square move acts on this local chart. Positive word becomes available when a verified sequence of adjacent positive moves from the stage’s starting word realizes the current mutation. The full-graph view checks the entire ice quiver against the paper. After deletion the explorer independently rebuilds the plabic graph at the new partial target.

In Leftmost mode, native label (i,k) corresponds at each completed stage to the kth omitted i from the left. In Rightmost mode, count from the right instead. The marker highlights the chosen word position in either convention.

Match checks the same word positions, frozen vertices and weighted arrows incident to a mutable vertex. On the paper view, Frozen edges also shows the frozen–frozen entries, including halves, retained during mutation. Agreement is checked for the displayed input and stage.
